# Liquidity Provider

When a trader places an order through a broker the market needs enough buying and selling volume to complete that trade at a stable price

This available buying and selling volume is known as market liquidity

The organisations that help provide this liquidity are called liquidity providers

![Liquidity Provider](/learning/liquidity-provider.svg)

---

## What Is a Liquidity Provider

A liquidity provider is a large financial institution or professional trading firm that helps keep financial markets active

Liquidity providers hold large amounts of financial assets and continuously offer both buying prices and selling prices

By remaining ready to trade they help other market participants enter or exit positions quickly and efficiently

Examples of liquidity providers include

* Global banks
* Hedge funds
* High frequency trading firms
* Market making institutions

---

## How Liquidity Providers Create Market Depth

Many brokers rely on liquidity providers to connect their retail clients with the wider financial markets

A broker may receive pricing from several liquidity providers and present competitive prices to its traders

When a trader places an order

1 The broker routes the order to its pool of liquidity providers

2 A liquidity provider that is willing to trade at the available price may take the other side of the transaction

3 The order is executed using the available liquidity

This process helps traders receive fast and efficient trade execution

---

## The Difference Between Brokers And Liquidity Providers

It is important to understand that brokers and liquidity providers perform different roles

A broker gives traders access to the market through trading platforms accounts and execution services

A liquidity provider helps supply the buying and selling volume that supports market activity

Although brokers and liquidity providers often work together they are not the same

---

## Key Takeaway

Liquidity providers play an important role in helping financial markets function efficiently

They contribute buying and selling volume that helps traders execute orders with greater speed and more stable pricing

Without sufficient liquidity trading could become slower prices could change more sharply and trading costs could increase
